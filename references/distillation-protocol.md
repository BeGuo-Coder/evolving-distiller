# Distillation Protocol

## Goal

Convert source material into a compact executable Skill whose runtime instructions remain traceable through claims and models back to evidence.

## Evidence classes

Prioritize:

1. belief formation,
2. decision record,
3. failure/correction,
4. contradiction/tension,
5. boundary statement,
6. repeated reasoning habit,
7. generic opinion only when it anchors a stronger class.

## Evidence record questions

Every high-value record should answer:

- What is the extracted claim?
- What source supports it?
- Is support direct or inferred?
- What context limits it?
- Is there counterevidence?
- What reusable mechanism follows from it?

## From evidence to claims

Do not place raw evidence directly into runtime rules. First form concise claim nodes that can be challenged, weakened, or contradicted independently.

Prefer one claim per proposition. If an evidence item supports several propositions, create several claims with separate IDs.

## From claims to models

Promote a candidate mechanism to a core model only when it is sufficiently:

- recurrent across contexts,
- generative on new problems,
- distinctive rather than generic.

Use 3-7 core models by default. More usually means insufficient compression.

## Contradiction handling

Classify contradictions as:

- temporal: earlier vs later position,
- contextual: different domains use different rules,
- value tension: two principles remain in conflict.

Never repair a contradiction by inventing a synthesis. Encode the conditions and evidence for each side.

## Multi-lens synthesis

Keep extraction and synthesis separate. Recommended sequence:

1. provenance and chronology,
2. beliefs/decisions,
3. failures/contradictions,
4. mental models,
5. skeptical challenge,
6. synthesis and graph construction.

Use independent agents when available. Otherwise perform separated passes and record the limitation.

## Copyright and corpus handling

Do not bundle full books, long transcripts, or scraped archives into the final Skill. Store source metadata, summaries, structured evidence, compact verification excerpts, claims, and graph links.
